# Trademark policy

The code is Apache-2.0. The name is not.

Section 6 of the Apache Licence grants no rights to the licensor's trade
names, trademarks or product names, and this policy says what that means in
practice — what you may call things without asking, and what you may not.

The point is not to restrict use of the software. It is that a user who
downloads something under this name should get this software, from this
project, unmodified. That guarantee is the only thing a name can carry, and
it is worth keeping.

## What this covers

The project name, the names of its binaries and container images, any logo
it adopts, and any distinctive name the project takes later. This policy
follows the name; it is not tied to the spelling it has today.

## What you may do without asking anyone

- **Run it, fork it, modify it, sell services around it.** That is the
  licence, and this policy does not touch it.
- **Redistribute it unmodified under its own name** — packages, container
  images, Helm charts, mirrors.
- **Say what is true.** "Compatible with", "works with", "built on", "a
  managed X service", "we migrated from Y to X". Comparisons, benchmarks,
  criticism, conference talks, books, courses, blog posts. Describing your
  relationship to the project accurately never needs permission.
- **Use the name in the name of a plugin or an integration**, in the
  conventional form: `x-format-pypi`, `terraform-provider-x`,
  `prometheus-x-exporter`. Not `X Enterprise`, not `X Cloud`.

## What needs permission

- **A modified build under the project's own name.** Patch it and ship it as
  the same thing, and the name stops meaning anything. Rename it, or say
  plainly and visibly that it is an unofficial build and how it differs.
- **Naming your product, company, service or fork after it**, or a name close
  enough to be confused with it.
- **Domain names, social accounts and app-store listings** built on the name.
- **The logo**, other than to refer to the project itself.
- **Merchandise.**

## Forks

Fork it — that right is not negotiable and this policy does not touch it.
Give the fork its own name. A fork under the original name is the one thing
that makes the name useless to everybody, including the fork.

You may say what your fork is: "a fork of X" is accurate and welcome. "X" is
not.

## About the name

A *fondaco* was the building where a medieval port received foreign merchants:
warehouse, customs house and lodging in one. Goods arrived from far away, were
inspected and weighed, were stored, and were released under the rules of the
city rather than the rules of wherever they came from. The Fondaco dei
Tedeschi in Venice is the one still standing. Ports that traded with each
other each had their own.

The word was chosen because it is distinctive rather than descriptive. That is
not decoration: a descriptive name — "package registry" — cannot be protected,
because nobody may be stopped from describing their software accurately, and a
policy written around one would have nothing to say. This one can be
protected, which is what makes the paragraphs above mean something.

## Asking

Open an issue. Requests that boil down to "we want users to know this is
really your software, unmodified" are granted; requests that boil down to
"we want users to think our thing is yours" are not.

## If this policy and the licence ever disagree

The licence wins. Nothing here restricts a right the Apache Licence grants
you over the software itself.
